Not sure what a term means? Quick glossary, no jargon

ROI (Return on Investment)

How much money the strategy made. +329% means a $100,000 starting portfolio grew to $429,134 after the strategy’s 1,013 trades over 6 years.

Win rate

The share of trades that made money. 68.9% means 698 out of 1,013 trades finished with a profit.

Profit factor

Total dollars won ÷ total dollars lost. Above 1.0 means the strategy makes more than it loses; above 2.0 is considered very strong.

Sharpe ratio

A “reward per unit of risk” score. Higher is better — 0.9+ is strong for a stock strategy, and this is the average return per trade compared with how much the returns bounce around.

Max drawdown

The worst dip your portfolio would have experienced along the way. 33% means the portfolio fell 33% from its peak at the worst moment before recovering.

Entry price / Take-profit / Stop-loss

Entry is the price to buy near. Take-profit is the price where the trade locks in gains (+35%). Stop-loss is the price where the trade cuts losses (−12%) so a bad trade can’t run away from you.

Confidence score

How strong the insider signal is (70–95%). A 95% signal means a CEO or CFO personally bought $1M+ of their own company’s stock on the open market.

SEC EDGAR Form 4

The official US government filing that insiders (CEOs, CFOs, directors, 10% owners) must submit within two business days of every trade they make in their own company’s stock. Every signal on this page links back to the original filing.
